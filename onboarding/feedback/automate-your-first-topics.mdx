## Overview

You don't automate the agent as a whole. You automate topic by topic, starting with the ones that already go well. Everything you have not automated stays a suggestion for your team.

**What you achieve:** your first protocols send replies on their own.

**Time needed:** about 2 minutes per protocol, once a topic is ready.

## When is a topic ready?

- Your team sends the suggestions for that topic without changes, ticket after ticket.
- Your **direct resolution rate** on [Analytics](/analytics) is rising. Watch it rather than your automation rate in the first weeks, and look at the trend across weeks rather than the daily number.
- The topic is simple, common and low risk. Good first candidates are order status, return instructions or unsubscribing from the newsletter. Topics involving money, such as refunds, usually come last.

<Tip>
  Not sure which topics are ready? Ask the [AI Manager](https://gominimal.ai/dashboard/ai-manager). It can check your ticket history and point out where automation is safe to switch on.
</Tip>

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Automate the protocol">
    Open the protocol in the [Training Center](https://gominimal.ai/dashboard/training-center), tick **Automated** at the top right and **Publish** the new version. An automated protocol shows a yellow **automated** label.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch on global automation">
    Once, open your agent in [Agents](https://gominimal.ai/dashboard/agents), go to **Automation**, enable automation and click **Update**. This unlocks automation for the agent. On its own, it sends nothing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch the first automated replies">
    Check the tickets on that topic for the next few days. Keep giving feedback on anything that is still not right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the next topic">
    Automate one or two topics at a time. Move on once the previous ones run well.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How the agent decides to send

A reply is only sent on its own when all of these are true:

1. Global automation is on for the agent.
2. Every protocol the reply uses is set to **Automated**. A message about two topics needs both protocols automated.
3. Any action in the reply is automated too. See [step 4](/onboarding/actions/add-test-and-automate-actions).
4. No guardrail trips, for example an angry customer or a customer asking for a person.

If one of these fails, the reply stays a suggestion. **Active** means the agent may use a protocol, **Automated** means it may send the reply.

<Note>
  Your agent also has an **automation prompt**, a second way to allow whole categories of replies. Leave it empty if you want to automate protocol by protocol. See [Automation](/training/automation).
</Note>

<Check>
  **Done when:** your first one or two topics are automated, and their replies go out without your team stepping in.
</Check>

## Common pitfalls

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A ticket was not automated">
    Open the insights page of the ticket and read the **Automation Coverage** section. It shows which layer held the reply back. Common reasons are a second matched protocol that is not automated, an action that is not automated, or a guardrail.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Lines that hold automation back">
    Remove instructions such as *"if the customer has further questions, escalate"* from your protocols. The agent already hands over when it cannot answer. Steps that depend on information no connected system provides have the same effect.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Automating part of a topic">
    You don't need to split a protocol. Add an **Escalate to Human** step on the path your team should handle. Only conversations that reach that step go to your team.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  To pause automation, for example over a weekend, switch off automation on the agent. Your protocol settings stay as they are.
</Tip>
