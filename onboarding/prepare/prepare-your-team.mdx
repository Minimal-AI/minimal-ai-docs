## Overview

The Prepare step makes sure nothing holds you up once you start training the agent. It has three parts: this page (your team and your knowledge), [connecting your systems](/onboarding/prepare/connect-your-systems) and [starting your subscription](/onboarding/prepare/start-your-subscription).

**What you achieve on this page:** a clear owner, a list of the topics to start with, your existing knowledge in one place, and all customer mail arriving in your helpdesk.

**Time needed:** about an hour, spread over a few days.

## Before you start

- You have received the invitation to your Minimal environment by email. If you cannot find it, search your mailbox for Minimal, it can land in spam.
- You know which helpdesk and webshop platform you use.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Appoint one owner">
    Choose one person who knows your customer service well and is not buried in daily tickets. The owner writes the protocols, processes feedback and decides how a topic should be handled. When several people train the agent separately, the tone of voice and the processes drift apart.

    Also involve a colleague who handles tickets every day. They know the exceptions, and they spot wrong answers first. If your customer service is outsourced to an agency or BPO, involve them too: their feedback is often what makes the difference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Block fixed time in the calendar">
    Plan about an hour for writing your first protocols, and 15 minutes a day for feedback once the agent is live. The teams that go fastest are the ones that keep this time free every day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the topics to start with">
    List the five to ten questions customers ask most. For most webshops these are order status and tracking, returns, delivery times, damaged or faulty products and stock.

    Don't know your numbers? Export your tickets of the last two or three months from your helpdesk and ask an AI tool such as Claude or ChatGPT to list your most common topics. Use the export to find topics, not as knowledge: old answers are not always right, and the reasoning behind them is missing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect what your team already knows">
    Gather the documents your team works from today: your FAQ and policy pages, standard answers or macros, internal work instructions, a brand book or tone of voice guide. Note the steps behind each answer as well, such as where your team looks up an order and when they hand a ticket over.

    You don't need to rewrite anything yet. In the Onboard step you explain each topic to the [AI Manager](https://gominimal.ai/dashboard/ai-manager) as if you were training a new colleague, and these documents are your input.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Route all customer mail through your helpdesk">
    The agent only sees what arrives in your helpdesk. If customers also mail a general info address or the personal address of a colleague, forward those to your helpdesk or merge them into one support address.

    The same goes for other channels. Social media, WhatsApp and marketplace messages are only picked up when they arrive in your helpdesk.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer customers only in your helpdesk">
    From now on, reply to customer mail in your helpdesk only, not in Gmail or Outlook. Replies sent elsewhere do not sync to the ticket, so the agent misses part of the conversation and gives wrong answers.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Check>
  **Done when:**

  - You have one owner with time in the calendar every day.
  - You have a list of your five to ten most common topics.
  - Your FAQ, policies and internal instructions are collected in one place.
  - All customer mail and messages arrive in your helpdesk.
</Check>

## Common pitfalls

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Waiting for complete documentation">
    Nobody has all customer service knowledge written down the first time. Start with what you have. Most teams learn the details while giving feedback in step 3.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Knowledge only in people's heads">
    If new colleagues are trained by listening and remembering, that knowledge has to be told to Minimal. Ask the colleague who trains new starters to join when you write the first protocols.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Uploading a full handbook or ticket export as knowledge">
    A handbook written for employees, or a pile of old replies, does not tell the agent how to decide. Copy what is relevant per topic and explain the steps instead.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
