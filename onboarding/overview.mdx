## Overview

Think of Minimal as a new colleague joining your customer service team. A new colleague needs access to your systems, an explanation of how you work, and feedback on their first replies before you let them answer customers on their own. Your onboarding follows exactly that order, in four steps.

Every step builds on the one before it. Protocols written without connected systems stay vague, and an agent that goes live with an empty Training Center writes suggestions your team will not trust. Follow the order and you avoid both.

## How long it takes

You set the pace. Some teams automate their first topics within a week, others take a few months. Most teams with an owner who has fixed time for Minimal get there in two to four weeks.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Week 1: Prepare and onboard">
    **Focus:** connect your systems, set up your agent and write protocols for your most common questions.

    **Outcome:** your integrations work, your main topics are covered and published, and you have tested them in the Playground.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Week 2: Go live and give feedback">
    **Focus:** set the agent to **active** so it writes suggestions on real tickets, and give feedback every day.

    **Outcome:** your team reviews suggestions in your helpdesk and the answers improve day by day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Week 3: Automate your first topics">
    **Focus:** keep processing feedback and automate the topics that go well.

    **Outcome:** your first protocols send replies on their own.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Week 4: Add actions">
    **Focus:** connect actions to your protocols, approve the first proposals and broaden automation.

    **Outcome:** the agent starts making changes in your systems.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  The agent never sends anything to a customer until you automate a topic. Until then, every reply is a suggestion that someone on your team reviews and sends.
</Info>

## Who you need

- **One owner.** Someone who knows your customer service well and has time for Minimal every day. This person writes the protocols and processes the feedback. One owner keeps the knowledge and the tone of voice consistent.
- **A colleague who handles tickets daily.** They know the exceptions and spot wrong answers first. They give feedback, they do not need to change protocols.
- **Admins of your systems.** Someone who can create API keys in your helpdesk, the owner of your Shopify store, and contacts at partners who manage systems for you, such as your warehouse or an agency.

## What separates fast teams from slow ones

- **One clear owner** for the protocols and the feedback queue.
- **Fixed time in the calendar.** Fifteen minutes a day is enough, as long as it is every day.
- **Integrations arranged early.** API keys from external partners are the most common delay. Request them on day one.
- **Internal alignment.** Where your team disagrees on how something should be handled, the agent will not be consistent either.

## What you can expect from Minimal

In the first weeks Minimal plans a regular session with you to check progress, after that the frequency drops. In between, create a case through the [AI Manager](https://gominimal.ai/dashboard/ai-manager) for anything the Minimal team needs to pick up, such as a custom integration or a new action. The ticket and context are attached automatically. You can also reach us through your shared Slack or Teams channel, or by email.

<Tip>
  The [AI Manager](https://gominimal.ai/dashboard/ai-manager) is your first stop for questions during the onboarding. It knows your setup and can explain what to do next.
</Tip>
