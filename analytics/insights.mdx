## Overview

The **Insights** page reads every customer conversation and describes it: what the customer asked about, whether the problem was solved, how they felt by the end, and whether the AI handed off to your team. It adds those descriptions up into trends.

You'll find it under **Analytics** at [gominimal.ai/dashboard/insights](https://gominimal.ai/dashboard/insights). To read the conversations behind any number, click it, or go to [Conversations](/analytics/conversations).

<Note>
  Insights is switched on per organization. If you see **Support Insights is not enabled for this organization**, contact Minimal to turn it on.
</Note>

## Conversations

A **conversation** is one customer thread: a ticket in your helpdesk, or a chat with the storefront agent. It includes every message on that thread, whether it came from the customer, the AI or your team.

- **Counted by start day.** A conversation belongs to the day its first message arrived (UTC), so a ticket that runs over several days is counted once.
- **Kept for 30 days.** Conversations are kept for 30 days after their last message, which is why you can look back at most 30 days.
- **Tickets and Storefront are separate.** Helpdesk tickets and storefront chats behave differently, so you see one at a time.

By default you see only conversations where **Minimal took part**, meaning one of your agents drafted or replied at least once. That includes drafts a human sent as their own. Switch to **All messages** to include every conversation, or pick a single agent.

## Status

**Status** is where a conversation stands now, based on its closing messages:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Resolved** | The customer's problem was solved |
| **Unresolved** | The conversation closed without solving the problem |
| **Open** | Nothing has settled it yet, or it went quiet without a clear outcome |

## Sentiment

**Sentiment** is how the customer felt about your business by the end of the conversation, not how polite or loud they were.

| Sentiment | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Positive** | They left happy: praise, thanks, or confirmation that the problem was solved |
| **Neutral** | A question asked and answered, with nothing said about how it went |
| **Negative** | They left unhappy: an order, product or charge that went wrong, or support that didn't help |

A calm request for a refund after a month of waiting is **negative**. A frustrated customer whose problem was then solved can end **positive**.

## Topics

A **topic** is a type of request your customers make, such as "Where is my order" or "Change delivery address". Topics are **discovered automatically** from what customers actually write, so you don't have to set them up.

- Each customer request is filed under the topic it matches. A conversation can raise several topics.
- A request that fits no existing topic goes under **Other**. When enough similar requests build up, they become a new topic, so new problems surface on their own.

## Tags

A **tag** is a label from a fixed list set up for your organization, such as "Claim delay" or "Damaged on arrival". The AI reads each conversation and assigns the tags that apply. A conversation can carry several tags, or none.

Topics show what customers ask about. Tags flag the things you have chosen to track. To change your tag list, contact Minimal.

## Protocols

A conversation **ran a protocol** when the AI or a human actually followed one of your [protocols](/training/protocols) in it. A protocol that was shown to the agent but not followed doesn't count.

## Escalations

An **escalation** is when the AI decides a person is needed and hands the conversation to your team. **Awaiting a human** counts escalated conversations that no human has replied to yet. A human draft doesn't count as a reply until it is sent.

To see why a conversation was escalated, open it in [Conversations](/analytics/conversations#escalations).

## First response time

**First response time** is the time from the customer's first message to the first reply they could see. It is measured separately for conversations where the AI replied first, and for those where a human replied first using an AI draft.

## Unusual days

**Days that stand out** compares each topic, tag or protocol with its own normal level, so a small topic that suddenly doubles is as visible as a large one. Here a conversation is placed on the day of its **last** message, because that's when its outcome is known. Today is still filling in and is marked as provisional.

<Info>
  Status, sentiment, tags and escalation reasons are AI assessments. Read them as trends across many conversations, not as exact figures for any single one.
</Info>
